The project

I created Neon Riff independently for my UI/UX Design course. Inspired by Guitar Hero, the project explores the screens and interactions of a rhythm game through an interactive prototype.

Evaluation and next steps

My professor reviewed the project. No usability sessions with players have been conducted. A future evaluation could examine whether new players understand the controls, navigate the menus, and interpret feedback during play.

Preparing the player

I requested a three-second countdown before notes appear, giving players a moment to prepare. I also specified fewer active lanes and a slower note speed for easier difficulty levels.

Making notes easier to distinguish

I requested more separation between notes, including on Expert difficulty, to reduce visual crowding. These changes document my design intent; their effect on player performance has not been measured.

A portfolio walkthrough verified navigation from the main menu to song selection, selection of Easy difficulty, the opening countdown, and the results screen after a no-input run. This was a functional walkthrough, not a usability study or a complete test of timing, audio, scoring, and every menu. What the application does

Loads images or camera captures, preprocesses images for Tesseract, runs either engine, and displays extracted text with bounding boxes. The original version records confidence and runtime and saves the selected output.

What the saved examples reveal

The receipt transcription matches the visually checked reference after whitespace normalization. The handwritten output includes errors such as “you” becoming “yov” and “$2.10” becoming “$2,|0”. Detecting a text region does not guarantee a correct transcription.

Evaluation preserves case and punctuation and collapses whitespace. Word errors also include spacing differences. The handwritten quantity was transcribed as “(1lb)” and needs confirmation. The receipt reference was visually checked, not independently double-annotated. Only one engine’s transcription per image was retained, so these results cannot establish which engine is more accurate.

A subsequent revision

A separate revision adds reference-based character and word error rates, keeps both engines’ transcriptions and annotated images, and separates preprocessing and OCR timing. It removes the automatic winner based on confidence. Eight calculation and export tests passed; full GUI and OCR validation remains pending.

Outputs prepared for the next run

  • Separate text files and annotated images for Tesseract and EasyOCR.
  • A metrics CSV with reference-based errors and timing for each engine.
  • A reference transcription and a record of the run’s settings.

The revised two-engine run has not yet completed because the original environment cannot load NumPy. No revised accuracy scores, speed improvements, or additional experimental results are claimed.
